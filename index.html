Expansiveness

I. Human, but more. We currently have access to only a fraction of what our minds are capable of.

prelude Contextualize yourself.

Human minds are bigger than they seem. They can contain a million songs, the smell of the moon, abstractions over abstractions.

We've been habituated to a vision of the future based on a narrow image of the past.

Looking to the deep past tells a different story. What appears is the absolute, dizzying expansion of human minds. We came from the formless expanse of the jungle, and yet we've conceived of Euclidean geometry. The idea of computing. Symmetry. Out of nothing, we've invented classical music.

To think of horse -> car is to think of more speed and mechanization; to think of ape -> thinking ape is to be in awe, and to think of a future of unknowable beauty. We hold that this is the truest nature of the future.

scherzo

Our minds are extremely general. Nothing has changed about our biological brains in the transition from prehistoric man to now, and yet we possess new, inconceivable structures: language. Language has been one of the biggest drivers of the transition from ape to thinking ape.

We believe this will happen again; that our current condition—running on words and hands—is far shy of what our minds can do.

Have you ever tried multiplying roman numerals? It’s incredibly, ridiculously difficult. That’s why, before the 14th century, everyone thought that multiplication was an incredibly difficult concept, and only for the mathematical elite. Then arabic numerals came along, with their nice place values, and we discovered that even seven-year-olds can handle multiplication just fine. There was nothing difficult about the concept of multiplication—the problem was that numbers, at the time, had a bad user interface.
Bret Victor

Consider that everything in the world is at the level of roman numerals; that most things are not truly difficult, and that a direct interface between the mind and the world would render them so.

This telepathic interface has some astonishing experiences. You'll be able to order an Uber in the same way as moving your index finger; the connection between you and your tools is so seamless that it is as if you can hold a whole codebase, genome, dataset, company, etc. in your head.

“I can't believe we used to press buttons.”

Software Engineer, United States, 2028

The net effect is the arabic numeralization of everything. People want to create films, not drag files.

A corollary: if we had visibility into the human brain, we would have a way to teach humans effectively. By providing interesting data and iterating until the mind accessed the desired concept ("multiplication", "harmonic oscillator"). Learning is no longer a chance-ridden process, but a pattern you can deliberately drive. Anything that one human can do is now available to all humans.

“I can't believe we thought physics was elite knowledge.”

Software Engineer, United States, 2030

And this is only the beginning.

The above description of the telepathic interface is like describing language through its advantages, when its grace is more fundamental. What good is language? We live in it, we are steeped in it, we are it.

coda What is the meaning of this technology?

In the same way that we want to extend our lifespan and not die prematurely, we should want to extend our capacities.

There's another, subtler meaning. Understanding is coupled to love. We are the only species that can feel an intense love for an object, an idea, a thing that is outside of ourselves. Barbara McClintock comes to mind, who spent thirty years looking at chromosomes under a light microscope.

She called the Nobel "an extraordinary honor," adding, "It might seem unfair... to reward a person for having so much pleasure over the years, asking the maize plant to solve specific problems and then watching its responses."
